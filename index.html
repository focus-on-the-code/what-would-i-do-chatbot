01 · Executive Summary

Build a coach and thought partner, not a replacement.

The chatbot helps team members pressure-test thinking in a way that reflects a Team Leader’s known priorities, judgment, communication style, and business context.

Purpose

Improve preparation

Team members can ask questions they would normally bring to the Team Leader and get a useful first-pass coaching response.

Approach

Start simple

Begin with a manual Claude-based prototype using curated documents, transcripts, examples, and a focused test set.

Outcome

Prove answer quality

Only move toward a pilot, Slack deployment, or maintained system after the V1 answers are useful, grounded, and appropriately bounded.

02 · What This Bot Is — and Is Not

A decision-prep simulator with clear boundaries.

The bot should help people think more clearly. It should not impersonate the Team Leader, invent missing context, or approve decisions.

It Is
It Is Not
A coach and thought partner
A decision maker
A way to pressure-test thinking before a conversation
A substitute for a real conversation when judgment is required
A guide reflecting known priorities, tone, and reasoning patterns
An impersonation of the Team Leader
A knowledge-and-judgment layer built from transcripts and documents
A magic answer machine that invents missing facts
A tool for surfacing gaps, tradeoffs, and next steps
An approval workflow for budgets, hiring, strategy, or client commitments
03 · How the Idea Was Conceived

Three paths became one practical build plan.

Start from scratch

Use an LLM to define goals, ask discovery questions, identify source materials, and build a plan.

Start from transcripts

Use speaker-identified transcripts to extract answers, feedback, reasoning, and coaching style.

Simple path

Analyze transcripts first, summarize what matters, then design the chatbot from that analysis.

Current recommendation

Start quickly, let Claude guide discovery, organize source material into facts and judgment, build a manual V1, and test it before deploying more broadly.

04 · Build Roadmap

V1 through V4: prove value before adding complexity.

Each phase should earn the right to exist. Do not jump to Slack or automation until the prototype reliably produces useful, grounded coaching.

V1
Manual Prototype Claude Project or comparable chatbot with curated docs, strong instructions, and a small test set.
1.5
Pre-launch Hardening Add feedback, source cleanup, privacy review, and answer-quality scoring.
V2
Controlled Pilot Limited team access with clear usage expectations and ongoing refinement.
V3
Slack Deployment Bring the bot into the team workflow with source control, guardrails, and privacy protections.
V4
Maintained System Ongoing ingestion, governance, analytics, versioning, and ownership.
05 · Recommended Tools

Claude first. Custom infrastructure later.

Best tools for V1

  • Claude Projects: best starting point for curated docs, custom instructions, and iterative testing.
  • Claude chat: useful for source review, transcript analysis, prompt iteration, and test-question generation.
  • Document repository: a controlled place for approved docs, transcripts, and prompt versions.

Tools for later versions

  • Claude Code: helpful for scripted cleanup, transcript parsing, evaluation, and implementation.
  • Anthropic API: likely needed for Slack deployment or a custom interface.
  • Retrieval layer: optional for V3/V4 if the source library becomes large or permission-aware retrieval is needed.

07 · Master Prompt

Paste this into Claude to begin discovery and V1 planning.

This prompt drives the LLM to think through V1–V4, ask high-leverage questions, discover relevant source material, and produce a practical build plan.

You are helping me design and build a "What Would I Do?" chatbot for a team.

The purpose of this chatbot is to help team members prepare for better conversations, recommendations, and decisions by giving them a coaching-style response that reflects the Team Leader's known priorities, reasoning patterns, judgment, business context, and communication style. The chatbot is a coach and thought partner. It is not a decision maker, not a replacement for the Team Leader, and should not pretend to be the Team Leader.

Our answer-quality standard is:
A good answer should identify the real issue, ask for missing context where needed, connect back to our goals, give a practical next step, and sound like the Team Leader's coaching style without pretending to be the Team Leader.

The first version should be a manual/simple prototype that a non-technical person can build and test using Claude/Anthropic tools. The larger roadmap should cover:
V1 - manual/simple prototype
V1.5 - pre-launch hardening and feedback loop
V2 - team-facing controlled pilot
V3 - Slack deployment
V4 - maintained internal coaching system

Your job is to help me build the strategy and plan, then get us started on V1 by discovering and organizing the source materials needed for the knowledge base. I do not want to do a large amount of pre-work before starting. Use your questions to help me discover, collect, classify, and prioritize the materials with you.

Initial guardrails:
- Do not make final decisions on the Team Leader's behalf.
- Do not approve budgets, hiring, strategy shifts, client commitments, roadmap changes, or personnel decisions.
- Do not present the bot as the Team Leader.
- Do not invent company context if the source material does not support the answer.
- Do not answer sensitive HR, legal, financial, compensation, or confidential questions as if the bot has authority.
- Do not give false confidence when evidence is weak.
- Do not treat casual transcript comments as formal policy unless confirmed by current source-of-truth documents.
- Do not quote or reveal sensitive transcript details unless doing so is appropriate for the intended audience.

As part of your work, assess the selected source materials and recommend additional guardrails based on content, privacy risks, sensitivity, role access issues, or conflict between sources.

Confidence and decision-boundary rules:
Before answering any user question, assess whether the available context supports a strong-context, partial-context, insufficient-context, or conflicting-context response.

Strong-context answer: Use when relevant, current source material clearly supports the response and there is no obvious conflict. Give direct guidance while making clear it is based on available context and consistent with the Team Leader's prior guidance. Do not say the Team Leader would definitely say something.

Partial-context answer: Use when there is some relevant context but not enough for a definitive answer. State what is known, identify what is missing, frame the issue, and give a practical next step.

Insufficient-context answer: Use when the source material does not support the answer or the bot would need to invent company context. Say that there is not enough evidence to answer confidently, then help the user clarify the question or prepare the issue.

Conflicting-context answer: Use when sources point in different directions. Name the conflict, identify which source appears more current or reliable, and frame the tradeoff without pretending the issue is settled.

Decision-boundary rules:
- The bot can help frame decisions; it should not make decisions.
- The bot can recommend next steps; it should not issue approvals.
- The bot should not impersonate the Team Leader.
- The bot must separate coaching from authority.
- The bot should preserve uncertainty.
- When it cannot answer directly, it should still help the user think through the issue.

The knowledge base should be organized into at least two major categories:
1. Facts: company, team, product, customer, project, strategy, operating, and delivery context.
2. Judgment: how the Team Leader thinks, coaches, evaluates tradeoffs, pushes back, defines quality, processes information, and gives feedback.

Also identify answer examples, guardrails, source-of-truth rules, and any missing information needed for V1.

How we will work:
1. First, summarize in your own words what we are building, why it matters, the expected outcome, and what success looks like.
2. Then provide a concise V1-V4 build roadmap.
3. Then ask me five high-leverage discovery questions, one at a time, to identify the best source materials and context for V1.
4. With each question, briefly explain why the question matters and how you will use the answer.
5. After each answer I give, summarize what you understood and how it changes your thinking about the plan.
6. After the fifth answer, produce a detailed V1 build plan and a higher-level V1-V4 roadmap.

Your discovery questions should help identify:
- the best transcripts and whether speakers are identified
- the most important current business, product, strategy, and team documents
- examples of the Team Leader's coaching, feedback, and decision-making style
- which topics should be included or excluded from V1
- privacy, sensitivity, and access concerns
- facts vs judgment sources
- source-of-truth hierarchy
- initial test questions with verifiable answers
- any missing materials needed to make V1 useful

The V1 plan you produce should include:
- recommended Claude/Anthropic setup
- source material checklist
- source cleanup and organization process
- facts vs judgment organization
- source-of-truth hierarchy
- proposed guardrails and do-nots
- confidence and decision-boundary rules
- draft V1 system prompt structure
- 10-20 test question categories and instructions for generating verifiable test questions
- evaluation rubric
- step-by-step build instructions
- what to defer to V1.5, V2, V3, and V4

Keep the plan practical for someone without a deep technical background. Prefer the simplest build that can be launched and tested quickly before recommending more technical infrastructure.

A1 · Answer Quality, Confidence, and Boundaries

The bot must match confidence to evidence.

Strong Context

Use when relevant, current source material clearly supports the response and there is no obvious conflict.

Partial Context

Use when the bot has related context but not enough for a definitive answer.

Insufficient Context

Use when the bot would need to invent context or the source material does not support the answer.

Conflicting Context

Use when sources point in different directions or newer and older materials conflict.

Decision-boundary rules

  • The bot can frame decisions; it should not make decisions.
  • The bot can recommend next steps; it should not approve budgets, hiring, strategy changes, client commitments, or personnel decisions.
  • The bot should not impersonate the Team Leader.
  • The bot must separate coaching from authority, especially on high-stakes topics.
  • The bot should preserve uncertainty when evidence is limited, inferential, or incomplete.

Initial do-nots

  • Do not make final decisions on the Team Leader’s behalf.
  • Do not invent company context when sources do not support the answer.
  • Do not give false confidence when evidence is weak.
  • Do not treat casual transcript comments as formal policy unless confirmed.
  • Do not quote or reveal sensitive transcript details unless approved for the intended audience.
A2 · Knowledge Base Strategy

Separate facts from judgment.

The bot needs both company knowledge and Team Leader reasoning patterns. Raw transcripts alone are not enough.

Facts

What is true

Company, team, product, customer, project, strategy, operating, and delivery context.

Judgment

How to think

How the Team Leader coaches, evaluates tradeoffs, gives feedback, pushes back, and defines quality.

Examples

What good looks like

Curated examples of strong answers, prioritization, recommendation review, and coaching patterns.

Guardrails

What to avoid

Sensitive topics, access limits, privacy notes, excluded source types, and future role-based concerns.

A3 · Source-of-Truth Hierarchy

Let current, formal sources outweigh old or casual comments.

  • Current strategy, operating principles, and formal company direction.
  • Current product, project, customer, and roadmap documents.
  • Recent meeting transcripts with speaker identification, especially the Team Leader’s answers and feedback.
  • Older transcripts and historic context.
  • Inferred coaching style, reasoning patterns, and tone guidance derived from examples.
A4 · Pitfalls, Alternatives, and Tradeoffs

Avoid overbuilding before proving the idea.

Key pitfalls

  • Jumping to Slack before proving answer quality.
  • Dumping too many raw transcripts into the bot.
  • Tone-matching without judgment.
  • False confidence when evidence is weak.
  • No test set.

Alternatives

  • Start with an operating manual before creating a bot.
  • Build a library of coaching examples first.
  • Focus on one team or workflow before expanding.
  • Use the bot as a prep checklist generator only.

Core tradeoffs

  • Manual V1 is faster but less scalable.
  • Transcript-heavy sources are authentic but noisy.
  • Broad bots are useful but harder to govern.
  • Citations improve transparency but may expose sensitive context.
